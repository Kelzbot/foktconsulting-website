Corrective action plan template for Single Audit findings

Free Word and Excel download. Updated November 3, 2026.

Every Single Audit finding needs a corrective action plan, and you write it, not your auditor. This template follows what 2 CFR 200.511(c) asks for and adds the one column most plans leave out: the record that proves the fix works. That record is what your auditor samples next year.

What 2 CFR 200.511(c) requires

The auditee prepares the corrective action plan as a document separate from the auditor's findings. For each finding in the current year's audit, the plan names the contact person responsible for corrective action, the action planned and the anticipated completion date.

If you disagree with a finding, or believe no corrective action is needed, the plan must say so and give an explanation and specific reasons. The template has a separate section for that.

The plan goes to the Federal Audit Clearinghouse with the rest of your reporting package. Your federal agency or pass-through entity then reads it and issues a management decision on each finding 2 CFR 200.521.

How to fill in each column

Finding number and compliance requirement

Copy both from the Schedule of Findings and Questioned Costs. The requirement is the letter the auditor tested, such as B for allowable costs or I for procurement. If the finding repeats a prior one, say so here.

Root cause

Start from the cause in the auditor's write-up, then go one step further and name what broke in your process. "Staff were unaware of the policy" is rarely the real cause. "The purchase order form has no place to attach quotes" is something you can fix.

Corrective action planned

Describe what changes in how the work is done. "We will comply with 2 CFR 200.320" changes nothing, and the auditor will find the same gap. Name the form, the step, the review or the system change.

Contact person and completion date

Use a title the auditor can find on your org chart and a date you can meet. If an action runs from a date onward, write "from [date]" and plan a check after it.

Evidence that it works

This is the column that closes findings. The auditor samples transactions dated after your fix and asks for a record for each one. Name that record here, such as the signed comparison sheet, the dated SAM.gov check or the monthly reconciliation. Then make sure it exists for every item, not just most of them.

Why plans fail at the retest

A common reason a finding repeats is a plan that changed a policy and stopped there. The new policy is real, but nothing in the daily work produces the record it promises, so the auditor's sample comes back with the same exceptions.

A self-test fixes most of this. A month or two after the fix date, pull your own sample the way the auditor would and check each item against the evidence column. Correct what you find before fieldwork starts.
