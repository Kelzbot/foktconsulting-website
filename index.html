Why findings repeat

Most repeat findings aren’t a policy problem. The policy gets updated, but nothing changes at the approval step, and nothing records that the step happened. The auditor samples next year’s transactions and finds the same gap. We fix the step and the evidence, not just the manual.

  1. Your auditor reports a finding.Reported
  2. The policy manual gets updated.Done
  3. The approval step stays the same.Gap
  4. Nothing records that the step happened.Gap
  5. Next year’s sample finds the same gap.Repeat

A Findings Review fixes steps 3 and 4: the approval step and the record it leaves.

Findings we fix

Find the compliance requirement named in your finding.

  • A·B

    Allowable costs & activities

    Charges approved without support or an allocation method.

  • L

    Reporting

    Federal reports that don’t tie to the ledger; late FAC filings.

  • I

    Procurement & debarment

    Missing quotes, price analysis or SAM.gov checks.

  • C

    Cash management

    Draws ahead of expenses; no reconciliation.

  • E

    Eligibility

    Participant files missing documents or a second review.

  • N

    Special tests

    Program-specific rules such as sliding fee discounts.

  • Sec. II

    Internal control

    Segregation of duties and weak year-end close.

  • M

    Subrecipient monitoring

    No risk assessment, incomplete subawards.

Letters follow Part 3 of the OMB Compliance Supplement. Sec. II is the financial statement findings section of your Schedule of Findings and Questioned Costs.

Equipment, period of performance, matching and program income findings too. Ask on the call.

Common questions

Something else? Email kelvin@foktconsulting.com or book a 20-minute call.

Are you a CPA firm?

No, and that’s deliberate. We aren’t your auditor, so there’s no independence conflict. Your CPA audits; we do the remediation work: procedures, controls and evidence.

Will this close our finding?

Closure is decided by your auditor and your funder. We deliver the controls and the evidence they look for.

What exactly do we receive?

Five deliverables, as editable Word and Excel files plus PDFs, in a shared folder:

  1. Test map (Excel): what your auditor tests for each affected requirement under the OMB Compliance Supplement, compared with what you do now, with the gaps marked.
  2. Written procedures (Word): new or revised steps that close each gap, and the record each step leaves.
  3. Corrective action plan (Word): owners, completion dates and the evidence that shows each action works, in the form 2 CFR 200.511(c) requires.
  4. 25-item self-test (Excel): we design it, your team pulls de-identified items dated after the fix, and we test them and explain any exceptions.
  5. Funder status note (Word): a draft for you to send on your letterhead to your awarding agency or pass-through entity.

You also get three video calls: a 60-minute kickoff, a 30-minute review and a 60-minute handover. After handover, you can email questions for 30 days.

What if our findings cover more than one area?

$2,000 covers one compliance requirement (one letter code, for example I – Procurement) and every finding on it in your latest audit, repeats included. Each additional requirement in the same review costs $1,000, so two requirements are $3,000 and three are $4,000.

Can we charge this to our grant?

Consulting fees are generally allowable as professional service costs under 2 CFR 200.459 when reasonable and allocable, usually through indirect or administrative costs. Confirm against your award terms and budget.

Do we need competitive quotes to hire you?

Not under the federal rules. A Findings Review costs $2,000, below the federal micro-purchase threshold of $15,000. (2 CFR 200.1 and 200.320(a)(1) use the FAR figure, which rose to $15,000 on October 1, 2025.) You can make a micro-purchase without quotes if you consider the price reasonable and note why in your file. Your own procurement policy may set a lower limit, so check it. Either way, we’ll send a written quote and scope for your purchase file.

Will you talk to our auditor or funder?

Yes, with your written OK. We can join your call with the auditor or funder to walk through the test map and the corrective actions. We don’t represent you, sign anything for you, or argue questioned costs. The status note goes out on your letterhead, signed by you.

Will you sign our NDA, and do you need patient records?

Yes. Confidentiality is built into our engagement letter, and we’ll sign your mutual NDA. We don’t take patient records, Social Security numbers or other sensitive personal data. We work from de-identified samples or by screen-share.

How soon can you start?

We take on two reviews at a time, so each one gets full attention. If we’re full, we’ll give you the next start date on the call. Work starts when the deposit arrives.

What happens after handover?

You can email questions for 30 days after handover. We also check in a month before your next audit fieldwork.

Where do you work?

Remotely, from Abuja, Nigeria, with nonprofits anywhere in the US. Calls run Monday to Friday, 12–3 pm Eastern. Emails get a reply within one US business day.
