# What your auditor is likely to retest: Cash Management

**Why this finding keeps coming back.** Cash is drawn on a schedule or in round amounts, not against actual spending, and nobody compares the two afterwards. The policy says "minimize the time between drawdown and disbursement", but the drawdown form has no step that proves it. The auditor lines up each draw against the expenditures it was meant to cover. If a draw runs ahead of spending, or interest on cash on hand is not tracked, the finding repeats.

| What the auditor will pull and test | Evidence that closes it |
|---|---|
| **A sample of drawdowns**, each matched to the expenditures it funded. (2 CFR 200.305) | A drawdown request form that attaches the ledger report supporting the amount, signed by the preparer and a second person. |
| **That cash is drawn only as needed**, with as little time as practicable between receiving it and paying it out. (2 CFR 200.305(b)) | A cash position report at the date of each draw, and a written rule for how far ahead a draw may run. |
| **That the draws reconcile to expenditures** for the whole award period. | A monthly reconciliation: cumulative draws against cumulative expenditures, signed, with any difference explained and cleared. |
| **Written payment procedures.** (2 CFR 200.302(b)(6)) | A short cash management procedure: who requests, who approves, how often, how the reconciliation works. |
| **Advances held and accounted for correctly**, with interest on federal cash tracked and handled as the rule requires. (2 CFR 200.305(b)) [verify the current interest rules for your award type] | A log of interest earned on advance payments, the amount kept and the amount returned, with the remittance record. |
| **Program income** reported and used as the award allows. (2 CFR 200.307) | A program income ledger, matched to the report, with the use of each amount recorded. |
| **Refunds and excess cash** returned promptly. | A record of each excess or refund, the date identified and the date returned. |
| **A self-test.** | Every draw made after the fix date, matched to its support, zero exceptions, filed for the auditor. |

**A 30-day fix plan**

| Pull the draws the auditor tested and your corrective action plan. For each, find how far the draw ran ahead of spending. | Write the cash management procedure. Build the drawdown form with the ledger attachment and the monthly reconciliation template. | Run the next draw on the new form. Complete the reconciliation for the last full month and clear every difference. | Self-test every draw since the fix. File the evidence. Send your funder a CAP status note. |

**A corrective action plan entry auditors accept:** "Effective [date], the Grants Accountant prepares each drawdown request with the supporting ledger report attached, and the Finance Director approves it only after confirming the amount covers expenditures paid or due within [number] days. The Finance Director signs a monthly reconciliation of cumulative draws to expenditures. Evidence: drawdown forms with attachments, signed reconciliations and a self-test of every draw since the fix." Every entry names a person, a date and the evidence you will show. Use the number of days your own award or program allows.
