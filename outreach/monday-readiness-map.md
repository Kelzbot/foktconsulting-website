## Part 2: what you must know cold (Sunday evening and Monday, about 3 hours)

Read these in the eCFR (ecfr.gov, Title 2, Part 200), in this order. Each one answers a question prospects ask.

| Read | It answers | One-line answer to have ready |
|---|---|---|
| 200.501 | "Do we even need a Single Audit?" | $1,000,000 or more in federal spending in a fiscal year beginning on or after 1 Oct 2024. |
| 200.512(a) | "When is it due?" | The earlier of 30 days after the auditor's report or 9 months after year end. |
| 200.511 | "Who writes the CAP and the summary schedule?" | You, the auditee. Not the auditor. |
| 200.516 | "Why is this even a finding?" | What auditors must report: significant deficiencies, material weaknesses, material noncompliance, and questioned costs above the reporting threshold in 200.516(a)(3). |
| 200.84 and 200.410 | "Do we have to pay back questioned costs?" | Questioned isn't disallowed. The agency decides in its management decision, and only disallowed costs are repaid. |
| 200.521 | "When is the finding closed?" | The federal agency (or the pass-through) issues a management decision, normally within 6 months of FAC acceptance. The auditor then retests next year. |
| 200.520 | "Why does it matter if it repeats?" | Findings can cost them low-risk auditee status, which means more programs tested next year. |
| 200.518 | "Which grants will they test?" | Major program selection is risk-based and decided by the auditor. You can say "likely", never "will". |

Then the **Compliance Supplement** (OMB, latest edition):
- **Part 3:** the 12 requirements (A to N). For each letter you have a retest note for, read its "audit objectives" and "suggested audit procedures". Those are literally what the auditor tests.
- **Part 6:** internal control. Every finding has a control side, and this is where the fix lives.
- **Part 4:** look up the programs (Assistance Listing numbers) of the prospect you're emailing.

Re-read your own blog posts on the five C's, material weakness vs significant deficiency, and questioned costs. A prospect may have read them.

## Part 4: guardrails, so you never overpromise

- You are **not a CPA firm**. No audit opinions and no attest work, and you can't "clear" a finding. The federal agency's management decision does that, and the auditor's retest confirms it.
- Never say the auditor **will** pass the fix. Say what record each test looks for, and make sure it exists for every sample.
- Questioned-cost disputes with an agency are the client's call and may need their lawyer. You can explain the rules and build the records.
- Things that change, so re-check before quoting: the 2026 Compliance Supplement's status, the proposed Uniform Grants Regulation (blocked from being finalized before 11 Dec 2026), and which agency now holds former USAID awards.
- Facts in emails come from their audit report or the regulation. Don't invent numbers or results.
