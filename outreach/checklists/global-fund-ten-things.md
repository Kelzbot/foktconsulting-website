# Ten things a Global Fund reviewer or LFA checks first in a Principal Recipient's files

Draft. For a Principal Recipient (or a large Sub-recipient) of a Global Fund grant. The grant is governed by the Grant Agreement, the Global Fund Grant Regulations, and the guidelines the Fund publishes for budgeting, procurement, audit and reporting. The Local Fund Agent (LFA) verifies on the Fund's behalf, and the Office of the Inspector General (OIG) has the right to audit. `[verify]` marks items to check against the current guidelines and the grant's own terms before sending.

1. **The Grant Agreement and the latest approved budget.** The signed agreement, the performance framework, the detailed budget as last approved, and every approved revision. The LFA starts from the approved budget and works forward.

2. **Budget variances and approvals.** Expenditure by cost grouping and intervention against the approved budget, with the Fund's approval on file for any reallocation past the flexibility the budgeting guidelines allow. `[verify the current reallocation thresholds]`

3. **Support for sampled expenditure.** Invoice, proof of payment, approval, and the link to a budgeted activity. The Fund recovers ineligible expenditure, so the question for every sampled cost is whether it was budgeted, approved and documented. (Grant Regulations on use of grant funds and recovery.) `[verify]`

4. **The PU/DR.** The Progress Update and Disbursement Request, with financial figures that tie to the ledger and programmatic results that tie to source records, submitted by the date in the agreement. The LFA verifies both halves before recommending a disbursement.

5. **Procurement and supply.** Procurement that follows the Fund's procurement standards and the Principal Recipient's approved procedures, with competition records and justification where a method was not competitive. Health products through the required channels where the grant says so. `[verify which procurement rules your grant applies]`

6. **Sub-recipient management.** A capacity assessment before the subaward, a signed SR agreement that passes down the grant terms, SR financial and programmatic reports reconciled by the PR, verification visits, and follow-up on SR audit findings. The PR answers for SR spending.

7. **Asset register.** Equipment and vehicles bought with grant funds, with location, condition and user, physically verified and reconciled to the ledger, and the Fund's rules on transfer and disposal followed. `[verify]`

8. **Bank accounts and cash.** Grant funds in the bank account named in the agreement, interest tracked and treated as the agreement requires, cash advances to SRs liquidated, and monthly reconciliations on file.

9. **Audit.** The annual external audit done by an auditor acceptable to the Fund, on time, with management's response and the status of each prior-year recommendation. OIG audit reports and the agreed management actions, with evidence of implementation. `[verify the audit guidelines version that applies]`

10. **Conduct and controls.** The Code of Conduct for Recipients signed and applied, the Code of Conduct for Suppliers in supplier contracts, a working mechanism for reporting fraud, and a tax exemption or tax recovery process in place where the agreement requires it. `[verify]`

Items 3, 6 and 9 are where most ineligible expenditure is found, because each one is about evidence that a cost happened the way the budget said it would.
