# Ten things a CDC reviewer checks first in a non-US organization's files

Draft. For a non-US organization holding a CDC cooperative agreement (including PEPFAR funding through CDC). CDC awards are HHS awards. HHS applies the Uniform Guidance to its awards, with HHS additions, and the terms are set out in the Notice of Award and the HHS Grants Policy Statement. `[verify]` marks items to check against the current Notice of Award and the HHS regulations in force on the award's start date, since HHS moved its implementing rules for awards issued from late 2025.

1. **The Notice of Award and every amendment.** Each NoA, in order, with its terms and conditions, the approved budget, and any special conditions. Reviewers read the most recent NoA first and expect you to have done the same.

2. **Prior approvals.** Written approval from the Grants Management Officer for changes that require it under the NoA: key personnel, carryover of unobligated balances, budget changes past the limit, scope changes, subawards and contracts named in the terms. `[verify the triggers in your NoA]`

3. **Support for sampled costs.** Invoice, proof of payment, approval before the spend, and the allocation basis for shared costs. Every sampled cost has to be allowable, allocable, reasonable and documented. (2 CFR 200.403 to 200.405 as adopted by HHS.)

4. **Personnel and effort.** Records of time actually worked on the award, approved and reconciled to payroll, for every person charged. (2 CFR 200.430(i) as adopted by HHS.)

5. **Procurement.** Written procedures, competition records, cost or price analysis above the threshold, and an exclusion check on vendors and sub-recipients. (2 CFR 200.317 to 200.327 and 2 CFR 180 as adopted by HHS.)

6. **Equipment and supplies.** An equipment register with the required fields and a physical inventory reconciled to the ledger, and a record of supplies bought with award funds. (2 CFR 200.313 and 200.314 as adopted by HHS.)

7. **Sub-recipient management.** Risk assessment, a subaward agreement that passes down the terms, monitoring, and follow-up on audit findings. Many CDC-funded programs run mainly through sub-recipients, so this file gets the longest look. (2 CFR 200.332 as adopted by HHS.)

8. **Cash and the Payment Management System.** Draws that match disbursements, no excess cash on hand beyond the period the terms allow, interest handled as required, and reconciliations between PMS, the bank and the ledger. `[verify the cash rules in your NoA]`

9. **Reports.** The Federal Financial Report and the annual progress report, filed on time, with financial figures that tie to the general ledger. Late or unreconciled reports are the fastest way to a special condition on the next NoA. `[verify report names and due dates in your NoA]`

10. **Audit.** The audit the NoA requires for a foreign organization (not the US Single Audit), done on time by an independent auditor, with findings and the corrective action followed up, plus any site visit or monitoring letter from CDC and your written response to it. `[verify the audit requirement in your NoA]`

Items 2, 8 and 9 are where CDC reviewers find most problems in non-US files, because they depend on dates and approvals, not on whether the money was well spent.
