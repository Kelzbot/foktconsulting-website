# Ten things a USAID reviewer checks first in a non-US organization's files

Draft. For a non-US NGO holding a USAID grant or cooperative agreement. USAID applies 2 CFR 200 to non-US organizations through the Standard Provisions for Non-US Nongovernmental Organizations (ADS 303maa) and the award's special provisions. The award and its provisions control. `[verify]` marks items to check against the current provisions before sending.

1. **The award itself.** The signed agreement, the attached standard and special provisions, the approved program description and the approved budget, all in one file. Reviewers start by asking what version of the provisions applies to this award.

2. **Approvals in writing.** Every change that needed the Agreement Officer's prior approval, and the written approval for it: key personnel, budget transfers past the limit in the award, scope changes, subawards, equipment, foreign travel where the provisions require it. `[verify the triggers in your award]` (2 CFR 200.308 as applied by the award.)

3. **Support for sampled costs.** For a sample of charges: invoice or receipt, proof of payment, approval before the spend, and the basis for allocating the cost to this award if it is shared. Costs must be reasonable, allocable and adequately documented. (2 CFR 200.403, 200.404, 200.405.)

4. **Personnel charges.** Records that show the time actually worked on the award, approved by a supervisor, that reconcile to payroll. Budget estimates are not time records. (2 CFR 200.430(i).)

5. **Procurement files.** Written procurement procedures, evidence of competition at the level the threshold requires, a cost or price analysis above the simplified acquisition threshold, and a check that the vendor is not excluded. One file per purchase with the reason the method and the vendor were chosen. (2 CFR 200.317 to 200.327, 2 CFR 180.)

6. **Equipment register.** Items bought with award funds: description, serial number, source, title, acquisition date and cost, location, condition, and a physical inventory at least every two years, reconciled to the ledger. (2 CFR 200.313.)

7. **Subawards.** For each sub-recipient: a risk assessment before the award, a subaward agreement that passes down the required terms, monitoring records, and follow-up on their audit findings. The prime is responsible for the sub's compliance. (2 CFR 200.332.)

8. **Cost share, if the award has it.** The same documentation standard as award funds: verifiable records, allowable costs, valued correctly, not counted toward another award. (2 CFR 200.306.)

9. **Financial reports and cash.** Financial reports that tie to the general ledger, advances liquidated within the period the award allows, interest on advances handled as the provisions require, and bank reconciliations on file. (2 CFR 200.305, 200.328, the award's reporting provision.)

10. **The USAID-specific provisions.** Branding and marking carried out to the approved plan, foreign tax reporting submitted on time, screening of partners and vendors against the prohibited parties lists, and the audit required by the award for non-US organizations, with findings followed up. `[verify each against the current ADS 303maa provisions]`

If a reviewer finds the first three in order, the rest of the visit goes faster. If any of items 3 to 7 is missing for a sampled transaction, that is where findings come from.
